## What is Oshikatsu?

For those who don't know, oshikatsu is an activity where you show your love and support towards something or someone you love by spending time and/or money. It is a popular activity in Japanese fandom (though it's not exclusive to Japanese fandom!) and has similarity to Stan culture in the west. The difference is oshikatsu is more of a physical activity, whereas stanning is prevalent in online spaces.

Fans will do oshikatsu with various goals in mind. Some people are serious about making their favourites popular, some want to meet other fans, some want to introduce their favourites to non-fans, and some find happiness through doing oshikatsu.

Doing oshikatsu is very easy! All you have to do is bring a merch of your favourite character with you. You can take anything from photocards to dolls. I personally have brought plushies, cards, and acrylic items to my office cubicle, gone out with badges stuck and plushies attached to my bag, or gone out with the character printed on my shirt. The easiest starting point in doing oshikatsu is to take pictures of any of your character's merchandise anywhere or with a meal.

Fans may also post a collection of their merchandise on social media and decorate them on a dedicated space in a specific way. This is called a *shrine*. Shrines may be posted on social media during the character's birthday; this is commonly known as *birthday shrines*. Birthday shrines can consist of only specific illustrations of the character, or any merchandise as long as it has the character's face on them. Size may vary from a few photocards up to a huge collection. Some people may take it to the extreme by hiring a photographer to take photos of the shrine or renting a space such as a cafe, a photo studio, or a hotel room!

Oshikatsu is not limited to having physical products of your favourite characters. Participating in the creative aspect of the fandom, such as posting fan arts, writing fan fictions, and doing cosplays, counts as oshikatsu activities. Attending concerts, watching livestreams, consuming the character's media (by reading mangas, light novels, or watching anime) also count.

## Itabag

Itabag (痛バッグ) is a bag covered in various merchandise of your favourite character. The bag may be typically covered in badges, dolls, or photocards of a character or multiple characters of the person's interest. The name itabag comes from the Japanese word *ita*, meaning pain. It can be interpreted as either 'painfully embarrassing' or 'painfully costly'. Itabags usually have a transparent window at one side where you can put stuffs in for display. There are also specific itabags for dolls, from ball-jointed dolls to 20cm cotton dolls!

Why do I suddenly bring itabag up? It's because the itabag culture is closely linked to oshikatsu activities, as it involves spending money for goods of your favourite character and showing your favourites to the world.

Did you know that different sides of the world have different itabag cultures? In Japan, itabags are are often decorated with repeating badges. Repeating itabags show the person's dedication to the character and the lengths they have gone through to achieve the design. It's common for fans to buy badge boxes in bulk (because badges are mostly sold in blind packs) which will increase the chance of them getting their desired badges.

Outside of Japan, sourcing official merchandise can be very costly, so repeating badges are not common. Because of low availability, fans may display fan-made or unofficial merch in their bags. You'll find itabags more commonly decorated with badges of multiple characters and smaller enamel pins!

No matter how the bag is decorated, as long as it brings joy to the owner, then that's all that matters!

## My Oshikatsu Experience

I used to do oshikatsu with finding fellow Ensemble Stars fans in mind. I used to regularly attend Japanese culture festivals or anime conventions with itabag full of Yuzuru badges. At times I'd engage in conversations and exchange social media. Other times, I'd feel very embarrassed over the awkward conversation. Regardless of the outcome, I think it helps me get out of my comfort zone.

Nowadays, I do oshikatsu by taking pictures with my plushies and bringing my itabag with me just because I feel like it. Sure, people may look at me weird, but some may go out of their way to poke me on the shoulder and complimenting my bag... which can be a good start to a small talk! I love being enquired about who is on my bag and enquiring them back about their favourite media or character.

Not all oshikatsu experiences are great. I've had my moments of being jealous, resulting in the need of validation and overspending, which are absolutely not good for your mental health and wallet! Doing oshikatsu only to seek validation from other fans isn't good (speaking from experience).

In the end, oshikatsu is something that you should do for your own enjoyment. As I previously stated, there are no guidelines in doing so, so you can go wild! Take a photo of that photocard in the museum! Take your plushies out for a picnic! Be free to love your favourites your own way!!
